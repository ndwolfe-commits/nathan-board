3. WHAT WE HAVE LEARNED ABOUT HOW WE WORK TOGETHER

1. You verify, then accept. Every report gets checked against the live artifact; items get accepted or thrown back ("verification pass complete - items 1, 4, 5a accepted"). Consequence for me: nothing I report can be softer than the artifact, because you will see it.

2. Numbered batches, answered in your numbering. You send work as numbered lists and want per-item reports with the direct answer up top. That is now the default format.

3. Consent before mutation. Reading is always fine; changing anything - calendar, shared documents, money - waits for an explicit yes. We learned this on 30 Aug when the calendar cleanup ran ahead of a clear yes.

4. Ambiguity gets flagged, not resolved. When instructions conflict or the record is thin, I ask. Established twice, both times the hard way.

5. Your attention has a price. Roughly: a filed £150 refund is not worth your attention once it is in motion; a £500 failure is. Self-resolving items get followed silently on the Waiting page; only things needing a decision reach you.

6. You build trust empirically. Your words on 30 Aug: "As a scientist I'm trained to look for nuance and as I learn to trust you that will be a way for me to increase trust for you." The practical form: audit trails you can sample-check - change ledgers, reconciliation docs, before/after proof. Trust earned per claim, not per reassurance.

7. Written rules beat repeated instructions. Every failure above ended as a codified rule: the board spec, the delivery protocol, flag-contradictions, card fallback, the deploy boundary. The pattern you have set: fail once, write the rule, do not fail the same way twice.

8. You keep custody of the sensitive steps. The Apps Script deploy stays in your hands; I do not get your Google account through a shared browser. Boundaries like that are settled decisions, not obstacles to work around.

9. No sycophancy, no putzing, no playing expert. Verify instead of agreeing ("make sure you're not being sycophantic here"); go read-only and come back with a map; route specialist questions to the specialist - the pharmacist, not web pages.

10. You work in bursts at odd hours. WhatsApp is the channel, one thread, and "you're gonna have to remind me of all this tomorrow" is a standing pattern - next-day re-briefs are part of the service.

4. WHAT IS CHANGING AS A RESULT

  • Standing rules are written down and checked before acting; contradictions get flagged to you, never silently resolved.
  • Every commitment becomes a tracked item with an owner. No more intentions floating free - that was the DPD root cause.
  • Shared or approved artifacts stay read-only until you say change them.
  • Watches get unconditional hard-timed check-ins, not just change-triggered ones. A silent watch is treated as a failure, not as no news.
  • Deliveries get the full protocol at booking: verified address, phone and entry instructions on the parcel record, stakes flagged, a ping when the courier is about two minutes out.
  • Specialist questions get routed to the named authority.

The honest summary: the useful half is real and compounding - the board, the errands, the research-first pattern, the daily layer around the boys. The frustrating half has a single root: I moved faster than your trust had reason to go. Every fix so far has been a rule that slows the risky half down without slowing the useful half. That is the direction.
